How it works

Photos and videos are multiplied by their size and added up in decimal units (1 TB is 1,000 GB, the way drives and cloud plans are sold). Immich's extra files are added as a percentage on top. Growth per year is your monthly additions times twelve, with the same percentage. The iCloud+ rows pick the smallest of Apple's US plans that fits: 50 GB at $0.99, 200 GB at $2.99, 2 TB at $9.99, 6 TB at $29.99 and 12 TB at $59.99 a month, read from Apple's iCloud+ page on November 6, 2026, before local tax. The disk row multiplies the terabytes you will need in five years, rounded up to a tenth, by your price and the number of copies.

These are estimates. File sizes vary a lot with the camera, its settings and the content, and the disk costs only what you enter. A drive also has some formatting overhead, and a backup copy kept off-site is worth more than a second disk next to the first one.

Questions

How big is one photo?

It depends on the camera and format. A 12-megapixel phone photo is usually 2 to 4 MB, a 48 or 50-megapixel one 5 to 8 MB, a mirrorless camera's JPEG 8 to 15 MB and a RAW file 25 to 60 MB. The presets are middle-of-the-road guesses; the quickest way to get your own number is to divide the size of a folder of your photos by the number of files in it.

How big is a minute of video?

Even more variable: it depends on resolution, frame rate, codec and the phone. The presets are rough typical values (a minute of 1080p at 30 frames per second is somewhere between 60 and 130 MB, 4K at 30 frames per second roughly 170 to 375 MB, 4K at 60 roughly 400 MB and up), so measure a few of your own clips and type the number in.

Why does the calculator add 15 percent for Immich?

Immich keeps your original files untouched and also stores small thumbnails and larger previews for photos and, depending on your settings, re-encoded copies of videos. Its documentation describes these files but doesn't give a ratio, so 15 percent is our rough assumption for a photo-heavy library. Change it to match what you see on your own server; a video-heavy library with transcoding on can need more.

Does it send my numbers anywhere?

No. Everything is computed in JavaScript on this page, and nothing you type is uploaded or stored.

Are the prices current?

The iCloud+ prices are Apple's US monthly prices as read from Apple's iCloud+ page on November 6, 2026, before any local tax. Prices change and differ by country, so check Apple's page before you decide. Other cloud services are left out on purpose: their prices differ by country and plan and we could not verify them for this page. Use the last field to compare any plan you pay for.
